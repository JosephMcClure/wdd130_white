Build a simple plant care routine

Plant care is easier when you understand what you are checking for. Start with water, light, and drainage. These needs are connected: a plant in a dim room may use water more slowly than one in brighter light. The size of the pot and the time of year can also affect how quickly the soil dries.

Check before you water

Check the potting mix before adding water. Follow the drying guidance for your particular plant instead of using the same schedule for every plant. When it is time to water, wet the mix thoroughly until water comes out of the drainage holes. Let the pot drain and empty the saucer afterward. Do not leave the bottom of the pot sitting in water.

Find the right light and pot

Indirect light is daylight that reaches the plant without strong sun shining directly on its leaves. A spot near a bright window can work, depending on the direction of the window and the plant's needs. Avoid moving a plant suddenly into strong sun. Watch for changes after moving it and adjust its position if needed.

Choose a container with drainage holes and a potting mix made for container plants. Drainage holes allow extra water to leave the pot. If you use a decorative cover pot, take the inner pot out to water it and let it drain before putting it back.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Watering too often: Feel the soil first and wait if it is still wetter than your plant needs.
  • Ignoring the light: Match the plant to the actual light in your room.
  • Changing everything at once: Check the conditions and make one adjustment at a time.

Yellow leaves or brown tips can have several causes, so look at the soil, light, and recent care before deciding what to change.
